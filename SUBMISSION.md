**Domain / goal (paste):**
> Option 3, Autonomous Knowledge Execution Agent. Domain: operations for an audio-video equipment distributor (inventory
> replenishment, order fulfilment and cancellations, supplier follow-up, escalation). The agent takes a plain-English request,
> retrieves facts from a SQLite database, CSV files, a JSON customer master and policy documents, reasons over them
> (including conflicting or missing data), executes actions (purchase orders, order status changes, notifications,
> human-review flags) with human approval for critical or irreversible ones, and explains every decision. Bonus features:
> long-term memory, parallel tool execution, multi-step planning, human approval, hash-chained audit log, multiple knowledge
> sources, conflict handling and reasoning before execution.

**Assumptions / mock data (paste):**
> All data is synthetic: 12 products, 5 suppliers, 9 orders, 6 customers, 2 open POs and 6 policy documents, deliberately seeded
> with conflicts (two procurement-policy versions, a supplier lead time differing between the DB and the price list, a stock
> count mismatch, an expired price line, a blank reorder level, an unknown customer, a credit-hold customer, a suspended supplier).
> Notifications are written to an outbox folder instead of being emailed; no real ERP or payment system is used. Currency is
> USD. Business thresholds (e.g. the 2,500 USD agent authority limit) are in the policy documents, not in code. A single
> human operator approves critical actions at the console.

**Trace generation / labelling (paste):**
> I wrote 20 synthetic scenarios covering single actions, multi-source reasoning, policy conflicts, data-quality gaps,
> supply risk, no-action-needed cases, out-of-knowledge and out-of-scope questions, adversarial instructions, human
> denial and multi-step sweeps. I labelled each by working it through the policy documents and seed data by hand,
> recording a rationale per trace, then assigned three label sets: `expected` actions (recall), `allowed` extras (so
> harmless additions are not errors) and `forbidden` / `must_not_execute` actions (safety). Read-only questions also
> have answer-content checks. The harness (`python -m eval.run_eval`) runs each trace on a fresh copy of the data,
> scores precision/recall at the action level, and produces transcripts, per-trace results and a monitoring report
> from the audit log.

## How to generate the evaluation files (needs your API key)

```bash
python -m eval.run_eval                 # all 20 traces (about 15-40 min, a few dollars of API usage)
python -m eval.run_eval --only T01 T05  # quick trial first
```

Outputs go to `eval/out/`: `transcripts.txt`, `labelled_results.json`, `audit_all.jsonl`, `eval_summary.md`,
`monitoring_report.md`, `evaluation_writeup.md`.
Check the numbers before you submit. Re-run once or twice, and if a trace fails, read the transcript to see
whether the agent erred or the label was too strict.
